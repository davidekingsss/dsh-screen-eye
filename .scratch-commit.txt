Let the model decide what a burst costs, up to the provider's own limit

The ten-frame cap was this plugin's guess, and the guess was wrong. It rested on
"the harness allows 20 images per message", which is true of one thing — a batch
passed to saveImages — and was applied to another. This plugin commits one image
per call, so that twenty never applied to it at all, and the real ceiling is the
DeepSeek adapter's 600 images per request: past it the harness replaces the extra
images with a text placeholder, so they would be captured and then not seen.

What actually bounds a sensible burst is the conversation, not a count. One image
costs at most 384 vision tokens (measured about 380 for a full-screen capture,
because the adapter projects every image to a 640,000-pixel budget whatever its
size), so ten frames is about 4k tokens and sixty about 23k. A plugin cannot see
the conversation a burst will land in; the caller can. The tool therefore states
the arithmetic in its own description — one image, at most 384 tokens, this is
what you are spending — and reports what it took, instead of deciding on the
model's behalf and then reporting a number it chose itself.

Where a count still decides something, it is headroom rather than a plan: a burst
whose ending is the picture's asks for STILL_HEADROOM_FRAMES (sixty) when the
caller named only an interval. Sixty rather than six hundred because the cap is
only ever spent on a picture that never settles, where sixty frames is 23k tokens
and six hundred would be conversation-ending. A caller that wants more of
something endless can say so.

Three more things had to move with it:

- the interval is documented as the lever, and duration_ms as a convenience that
  is explicitly not how a burst is sized — thinking in windows is the wrong way
  round for both cost and resolution;
- retention now prunes once at the start of a call rather than after every frame,
  which was safe while a burst was capped at ten and would otherwise have deleted
  the beginning of a longer burst and returned paths to files that were gone;
- timeout_ms is a per-call parameter, because the call's budget is the caller's
  to set. The harness enforces a tool's declared timeoutMs as a hard deadline and
  throws the result away when it fires, so the tool declares the ceiling it will
  accept (15 minutes) and validates against it, and a wait longer than the call's
  budget is clamped to the budget out loud rather than silently eating the
  capture's time.

Measured after the change: a thirty-frame burst over a 600x400 region, asked for
at a 20ms interval, came back as 30 frames at 32ms spacing covering 928ms in
1.3s, every path present. The old cap stopped at ten frames and 290ms.
